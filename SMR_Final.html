The Baseload Problem

  • Global electricity demand doubles by 2050 — and must decarbonize simultaneously
  • Solar and wind are intermittent — cannot guarantee power at 2am in January
  • Conventional nuclear is firm and dispatchable — but costs $20–30B and takes 15+ years
  • The grid needs something reliable, low-carbon, and buildable
Can we get nuclear reliability without the cost and complexity?

03

How It Works: Fission to Grid

1U-235 fission — neutron absorption splits nucleus; mass defect Δm → energy E = mc² · ~200 MeV per event
2Primary loop — fission heats pressurized water to 310 °C at 15.5 MPa; stays liquid under pressure (no boiling)
3Steam generator — helical tube bundle transfers heat to secondary loop; secondary water boils to steam
4Turbine — steam expands isentropically (state 3→4 on T-s diagram), spinning turbine shaft → generator
5Condenser — exhausted steam returns to liquid (state 4→1); pump restores pressure (1→2)
Rankine Cycle Thermal Efficiency
η = Wnet / Qin = [(h₃−h₄) − (h₂−h₁)] / (h₃−h₂)
NuScale: TH ≈ 310°C · TC ≈ 35°C · ηthermal ≈ 30–35%  |  1 kg U-235 ≈ 8,000 t coal equivalent
30–35% is the thermodynamic ceiling for these temperatures — not a design flaw, just physics

04

NuScale: Passive Safety

  • Every reactor produces decay heat after shutdown — must be continuously removed
  • Conventional plants: electrically powered pumps remove decay heat
  • Fukushima 2011: station blackout → pumps failed → decay heat → meltdown
  • NuScale: natural circulation only — hot coolant rises, cool descends, thermodynamic loop closes
  • No pumps · no external power · no operator action · self-cooling indefinitely
The safety case is physics, not procedure — NRC-certified on this basis (2020)

05

Why NuScale Leads

  • First and only SMR with U.S. NRC design certification (2020) — world's most rigorous nuclear regulator
  • Integral PWR: core + steam generator + pressurizer inside one sealed vessel — no external piping, no large-bore LOCA risk
  • Vessel: 20 m tall · 2.7 m diameter · shipped in 3 segments by truck, rail, or barge
  • 77 MWe per module · 250 MWt thermal · up to 12 modules = 924 MWe
  • NRC uprated design approved 2025 · regulatory momentum building
First-mover advantage backed by the world's toughest regulator

06

The Economic Reality

  • Capital cost: $6,000–$10,000 /kW — comparable to or worse than conventional nuclear per kW
  • LCOE: $65–90 /MWh vs. utility solar at $30–40 /MWh
  • Root cause: loss of economies of scale — large plants spread fixed costs over more MW; SMRs can't yet
  • UAMPS Idaho cancelled 2023 — LCOE reached $89/MWh, subscribers withdrew
  • FOAK cost risk is real, not theoretical — first projects carry the full learning burden
Economics are the barrier — not engineering. The technology works.

07

Barriers to Deployment

Regulatory
NRC design certification exists — but project-level licensing remains slow. Framework built for one-off large plants, not modular deployment.
Years per project · not designed for modularity
Supply Chain
Factory model doesn't exist at scale yet. Cost advantages remain theoretical until orders justify factory investment.
~10–15% cost reduction per doubling (estimated)
Market Competition
Solar/wind LCOE keeps falling while SMR costs stay flat. Need nth-of-a-kind economics to close the gap.
Solar: $30–40/MWh and falling · SMR: $65–90/MWh flat
All three barriers are interconnected — solving one helps the others. The first projects are the unlock.
08

Where SMRs Fit

  • Not a silver bullet — won't out-compete solar on cost
  • They fill a specific gap: firm, dispatchable, low-carbon baseload
  • No battery technology at scale fills this gap today
  • Engineering proven · Passive safety real · Regulatory path exists
  • Remaining barrier is economic — solvable if first projects get built
A specific tool for a specific gap — if the first projects get financed
