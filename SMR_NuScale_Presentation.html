Small Modular Reactors

Engineering, Economics, and the Case for NuScale

Energy and the Environment

Joshua Fixman  |  Professor Lovejoy

The Baseload Problem

  • Global electricity demand: 2x by 2050
  • Renewables are intermittent — no 24/7 guarantee
  • Conventional nuclear: $20–30B, 15+ years to build

Can we get nuclear reliability without the cost?

1 / 9

A New Manufacturing Model

  • < 300 MW, factory-built modules
  • Standardized components, shipped to site
  • Incremental deployment as demand grows

Not a smaller reactor — a different construction paradigm

2 / 9

How It Works: Fission to Grid

  • U-235 fission → E = mc² energy release
  • Heat → pressurized water → steam generator
  • Rankine cycle: 30–35% thermal efficiency

Not a design flaw — just physics

3 / 9

NuScale: Passive Safety

  • Natural circulation: no pumps needed
  • Gravity-driven cooling — no power required
  • Decay heat removed by thermodynamics alone

The safety case is physics, not procedure

4 / 9

Why NuScale Leads

  • First & only NRC-certified SMR design
  • Integral PWR: core + steam gen + pressurizer in one vessel
  • 77 MWe per module → 462 MW with 6 modules

Not a concept — a certified, deployable design

5 / 9

The Economic Reality

  • Capital cost: $6,000–$10,000/kW installed
  • LCOE: $65–90/MWh vs. solar at $30–40
  • Idaho project cancelled — FOAK risk is real

Loss of economies of scale is the core structural problem

6 / 9

Barriers to Deployment

  • Regulatory: licensing not built for modular
  • Supply chain: factory model unproven at scale
  • Market: solar/wind costs still falling

Nth-of-a-kind economics require the first plants to get built

7 / 9

Where SMRs Fit

  • Not a silver bullet — a specific tool
  • Firm, dispatchable, low-carbon baseload
  • Engineering proven; barrier is economic & solvable

The grid needs what renewables alone cannot provide
