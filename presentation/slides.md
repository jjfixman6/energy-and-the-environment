# SMR (NuScale) Presentation — Slide Structure & Visual Guide

## Slide 1: The Baseload Problem

**Title:** The Baseload Problem

**Bullets:**
- Global electricity demand: 2x by 2050
- Renewables: intermittent, no 24/7 guarantee
- Conventional nuclear: $20-30B, 15+ years

**Visual:** Split-screen infographic. Left: sun/wind icons with a dashed availability line (showing intermittency gaps at night/calm). Right: nuclear plant icon with a flat, continuous power line. Below both: a cost/time bar showing conventional nuclear's $20-30B and 15-year timeline. Use a dark background with contrasting accent colors (amber for renewables, blue for nuclear).

**Layout:** Title top-left. Three short bullets center-left. Infographic dominant right (60% of slide).

**Purpose:** Frame the energy trilemma — reliability vs. cost vs. carbon — to establish why SMRs matter.

---

## Slide 2: What Are SMRs?

**Title:** A New Manufacturing Model

**Bullets:**
- < 300 MW, factory-built modules
- Standardized components, shipped to site
- Incremental deployment as demand grows

**Visual:** Side-by-side comparison diagram. Left: "Conventional" — single massive containment dome, labeled ">1,000 MW", with icons for on-site custom construction (crane, workers). Right: "SMR" — factory building producing identical small reactor modules on an assembly line, truck shipping them to site, modules stacking together. Arrow showing "Factory → Ship → Assemble."

**Layout:** Title top-left. Bullets bottom-left. Comparison diagram spans center-right (65% of slide).

**Purpose:** Establish that SMRs are not just smaller reactors — they represent a fundamentally different construction paradigm.

---

## Slide 3: Fission to Grid

**Title:** How It Works: Fission to Grid

**Bullets:**
- U-235 fission → E = mc² energy release
- Heat → pressurized water → steam generator
- Rankine cycle: 30-35% thermal efficiency

**Visual:** Horizontal process flow diagram with labeled stages: (1) Uranium fuel rod icon → (2) Fission reaction (splitting atom graphic) → (3) Heat transfer to pressurized water (thermometer at >300°C) → (4) Steam generator → (5) Turbine → (6) Generator → (7) Grid/electricity icon. Each stage connected by arrows. Color-coded: red for heat stages, blue for water/steam, yellow for electricity.

**Layout:** Title top-center. Bullets bottom-left (small). Process flow diagram dominates center (70% of slide width).

**Purpose:** Demonstrate mastery of the underlying physics — fission energy conversion is not a black box.

---

## Slide 4: Passive Safety by Design

**Title:** NuScale: Passive Safety

**Bullets:**
- Natural circulation: no pumps needed
- Gravity-driven cooling — no power required
- Decay heat removed by thermodynamics alone

**Visual:** Labeled cross-section schematic of NuScale's reactor module. Show: (1) Reactor core at bottom (heat source), (2) Hot coolant rising (red arrows up), (3) Steam generator in upper region, (4) Cooled fluid descending (blue arrows down), (5) Containment vessel surrounding everything. Label the convection loop explicitly. Add a callout box: "Fukushima failure mode: pump power lost → NuScale: no pumps to lose."

**Layout:** Title top-left. Three bullets left (30%). Reactor cross-section schematic right (70%).

**Purpose:** This is the technical crown jewel — show that passive safety isn't marketing, it's thermodynamics.

---

## Slide 5: Deployment Readiness

**Title:** Why NuScale Leads

**Bullets:**
- First & only NRC-certified SMR design
- Integral PWR: core + steam gen + pressurizer in one vessel
- 77 MWe per module → 462 MW (6 modules)

**Visual:** Three-panel layout. Panel 1: NRC certification seal/badge with "2020 — First SMR Design Certification" label. Panel 2: Simplified cutaway of the integral pressure vessel showing core, steam generator, and pressurizer consolidated inside one unit (contrast with conventional design showing separate external components). Panel 3: Modular stacking diagram — 1 module (77 MWe), 3 modules (231 MWe), 6 modules (462 MWe) with scaling arrow.

**Layout:** Title top-left. Bullets across bottom. Three visual panels evenly distributed across center.

**Purpose:** Establish NuScale's unique credibility — not a concept, but a certified, deployable design.

---

## Slide 6: The Economic Reality

**Title:** The Economic Reality

**Bullets:**
- Capital cost: $6,000-$10,000/kW installed
- LCOE: $65-90/MWh vs. solar at $30-40/MWh
- Idaho project cancelled — FOAK risk is real

**Visual:** Bar chart comparison with three groups: (1) SMR capital cost vs. conventional nuclear vs. solar ($/kW), (2) LCOE comparison bars: SMR ($65-90), gas+carbon ($70-100), utility solar ($30-40), conventional nuclear ($90-140). Use red accent on SMR bars to draw attention. Add a small callout/annotation: "Loss of economies of scale: fixed costs spread over fewer MW."

**Layout:** Title top-left. Three bullets left (35%). Bar chart right (65%).

**Purpose:** Confront the economic weakness head-on — builds credibility by not overselling.

---

## Slide 7: Three Barriers Remain

**Title:** Barriers to Deployment

**Bullets:**
- Regulatory: licensing not designed for modular
- Supply chain: factory manufacturing unproven at scale
- Market: solar/wind costs still falling

**Visual:** Three-column layout with icons. Column 1: Regulatory — gavel/document icon with timeline showing lengthy licensing process. Column 2: Supply Chain — factory icon with question mark, labeled "nth-of-a-kind economics needed." Column 3: Market Competition — downward cost curve for solar/wind vs. flat SMR cost line, showing the gap SMRs must close.

**Layout:** Title top-center. Three columns evenly spaced below, each with icon + 1-2 line label. Clean, minimal.

**Purpose:** Show awareness of real-world deployment friction — demonstrates mature, balanced analysis.

---

## Slide 8: Where SMRs Fit

**Title:** Where SMRs Fit

**Bullets:**
- Not a silver bullet — a specific tool
- Firm, dispatchable, low-carbon baseload
- Engineering proven; barrier is economic & solvable

**Visual:** Conceptual energy mix diagram. Horizontal bar or pie showing a future grid: large solar/wind segment, storage segment, and a clearly labeled "Firm Baseload: SMRs" segment filling the reliability gap. Alternatively: a simple 2x2 matrix with axes "Dispatchable vs. Intermittent" and "Low-carbon vs. High-carbon" — SMRs occupy the "Dispatchable + Low-carbon" quadrant alongside hydro and geothermal, while solar/wind sit in "Intermittent + Low-carbon."

**Layout:** Title top-left. Bullets left (35%). Energy mix visual right (65%). Final line at bottom: strong closing statement in larger font.

**Purpose:** Land the nuanced conclusion — SMRs solve a specific constraint, and the path forward is economic, not technical.
